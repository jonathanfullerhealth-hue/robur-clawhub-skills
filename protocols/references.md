# Post-GLP-1 Conditioning Protocol — Annotated References

Supporting evidence for `post-glp1-conditioning.md` and the `glp1-triage` skill.
Annotated with what each source actually supports, so a reviewing clinician can trace any
threshold in the protocol to its origin.

Last reviewed: September 2026. Reassess every six months — see protocol §11.

---

## A. Assessment thresholds

**A1. Cruz-Jentoft AJ, Bahat G, Bauer J, Boirie Y, Bruyère O, Cederholm T, Cooper C, Landi F,
Rolland Y, Sayer AA, Schneider SM, Sieber CC, Topinková E, Vandewoude M, Visser M, Zamboni M,
et al. Sarcopenia: revised European consensus on definition and diagnosis.**
*Age and Ageing.* 2019;48(1):16–31. doi:10.1093/ageing/afy169
[Full text](https://academic.oup.com/ageing/article/48/1/16/5126243)

The source of every absolute cut-point in the protocol's assessment battery: grip strength
<27 kg (men) / <16 kg (women); 5× sit-to-stand >15 s; gait speed ≤0.8 m/s; appendicular lean
mass index <7.0 kg/m² (men) / <5.5 kg/m² (women). EWGSOP2 also establishes the
find–assess–confirm–severity sequence the battery follows.

**A2. Performance of the EWGSOP2 Cut-Points of Low Grip Strength for Identifying Sarcopenia
and Frailty Phenotype: A Cross-Sectional Study in Older Inpatients.**
[PMC8037004](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8037004/)

Supports using grip strength as the primary screening measure. Low grip by EWGSOP2 cut-points
was associated with impaired gait speed, timed-up-and-go, and frailty.

**A3. Handgrip strength rather than chair stand test should be used to diagnose sarcopenia in
geriatric rehabilitation inpatients (RESORT).**
*Age and Ageing.* 2022;51(11):afac242.
[Full text](https://academic.oup.com/ageing/article/51/11/afac242/6834150)

Basis for the protocol's decision to make grip the primary measure and treat a check-in
without it as incomplete, rather than substituting sit-to-stand.

## B. Body composition on GLP-1 therapy

**B1. Lean Mass and Musculoskeletal Preservation in GLP-1-Based Obesity Treatment: Nutrition,
Exercise, Supplementation, and Monitoring Strategies.**
*Metabolites.* 2026;16(6):364.
[Journal](https://www.mdpi.com/2218-1989/16/6/364) · [PMC13303403](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13303403/)

The protocol's central source. Supports the estimate that 30–40% of weight lost on GLP-1
therapy derives from fat-free mass, lean mass declines of 10–15% in patients losing >15% of
body weight, elevated risk in older adults and sarcopenic obesity, and the combined
protein-plus-resistance-training strategy.

**B2. Wilding JPH, et al. Impact of Semaglutide on Body Composition in Adults With Overweight
or Obesity: Exploratory Analysis of the STEP 1 Study.**
*Journal of the Endocrine Society.* 2021;5(Suppl 1):A16.
[Abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jes/article/5/Supplement_1/A16/6240360)

**Read this alongside B1 — the two are frequently conflated in popular coverage, and a
reviewing clinician will know it.** In this DEXA substudy (n=140; semaglutide 95, placebo 45;
mean weight 98.4 kg, BMI 34.8; 76% female), body weight fell 15.0% with semaglutide vs 3.6%
with placebo, and the *proportion* of lean body mass increased, because fat mass fell further
than lean mass.

Both statements are true and they are not in conflict: absolute fat-free mass falls
substantially, while relative body composition improves. The protocol's concern is absolute
functional capacity in patients with limited reserve — older adults, women, those with low
baseline muscle — not the body composition ratio. State it that way. Framing GLP-1 therapy as
uniformly damaging to body composition is not supported and will cost credibility with
prescribers.

## C. Resistance training during energy restriction

**C1. Energy deficiency impairs resistance training gains in lean mass but not strength: A
meta-analysis and meta-regression.**
[PubMed 34623696](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34623696/)

The basis for the protocol's central KPI decision. Strength gains are preserved in energy
deficit even where lean mass gains are blunted, which makes strength the most responsive and
most motivating measure available during active weight loss.

**C2. Lean mass sparing in resistance-trained athletes during caloric restriction: the role of
resistance training volume.**
[PMC9012799](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9012799/) · [PubMed 35146569](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35146569/)

Supports maintaining or increasing resistance volume through the deficit rather than reducing
it — the protocol's "volume goes up as the dose goes up" principle. Also the source for
avoiding energy deficits greater than ~500 kcal/day where preserving lean mass is the goal.

**C3. Roth C, et al. Resistance training volume does not influence lean mass preservation
during energy restriction in trained males.**
*Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports.* 2023. doi:10.1111/sms.14237
[Full text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/sms.14237)

**Included deliberately as a counterweight to C2.** Found no volume effect on lean mass
preservation in trained males. Read together, C2 and C3 support the protocol's actual
position: *some* resistance training is essential and even low volume protects lean mass,
while the case for high volume specifically is not settled. This is why the protocol sets
2×/week as a floor and escalates to 3×/week by risk tier rather than prescribing maximal
volume to everyone.

**C4. Resistance training as a key strategy for high-quality weight loss in men and women.**
*Frontiers in Endocrinology.* 2025.
[Full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2025.1725500/full)

Supports resistance training preserving and increasing fat-free mass during weight loss in
both sexes, and the sex differences noted in risk stratification.

## D. Protein

**D1. Suboptimal protein intake for hypocaloric diet needs while using glucagon-like peptide-1
receptor agonists.**
[PMC12419545](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12419545/)

Documents that GLP-1 users routinely fall short of protein requirements for their degree of
energy restriction. The empirical basis for treating a protein shortfall as a referral rather
than a coaching problem.

**D2. Macronutrient, Micronutrient Supplementation and Monitoring for Patients on GLP-1
Agonists: Can We Learn from Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery?**
[PMC12693348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12693348/)

The bariatric-surgery analogy the protocol is built on: comparable magnitudes of weight loss,
managed there with standardized multidisciplinary follow-up that GLP-1 therapy currently
lacks. Supports the 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day target, even distribution, and 20–30 g per feeding.

**D3. Bridging the nutrition guidance gap for GLP-1 receptor agonist therapy assisted weight
loss: lessons from bariatric surgery.**
[PMC12913018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12913018/)

Source of the care-gap figures: only ~20% of GLP-1 users report referral to a registered
dietitian, and ~51% report receiving guidance on managing GI side effects.

## E. Bone

**Corrected September 2026 against primary sources.** The earlier version of this section
rested on a secondary review and was flagged "trace to the primary trials before quoting."
Tracing it showed the claim did not hold: the 2–3% hip BMD figure overstates the effect, and
the assertion that dual agonists carry higher bone risk is unsupported and possibly backwards.
Both have been replaced. Retrieved via PubMed.

**E1. Liu Y, Walzer D, Schmitz S, Shukla AP, Ma X, Chirko D, Pipia I, Sathi S, Shukairy U,
Greenberg M, Kashyap SR, Stein EM. Skeletal effect of semaglutide and tirzepatide in patients
with increased risk of fractures.**
*Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism.* 2026;111(7):1959–1966.
[doi:10.1210/clinem/dgag052](https://doi.org/10.1210/clinem/dgag052)

The primary source for the protocol's bone position. Single-centre retrospective study, 255
semaglutide/tirzepatide users matched to 255 non-users by age, sex, BMI and diabetes status,
each with DXA before initiation and ≥6 months after. Median follow-up 17 months; median 5%
weight loss. **Both groups had significant declines in total hip and femoral neck BMD, of
similar magnitude.** Weight loss correlated directly with bone loss (r = 0.32 total hip,
r = 0.17 femoral neck, both P < .01). Among patients *without* diabetes, total hip loss was
greater in the GLP-1 RA group — **−1% vs −0.6%, P = .04**. Among patients with diabetes, loss
was comparable between groups.

The clinically useful reading: bone loss here is driven by weight loss, the drug-attributable
increment is small, and it appears only in the non-diabetic subgroup.

**E2. Anastasilakis AD, Paccou J, Palermo A, Polyzos SA. The effects of anti-obesity
medications on bone metabolism: A critical appraisal.**
*Diabetes, Obesity & Metabolism.* 2025;27(9):4674–4688.
[doi:10.1111/dom.16541](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.16541)

GLP-1R agonists show a neutral or negative but **not clinically significant** effect on bone
turnover markers and BMD in clinical studies, and **fracture risk does not appear to increase
at clinically relevant doses.** Dual GLP-1R/GIPR, dual GLP-1R/GCGR and triple agonists may
have a *positive* effect on bone, on limited and mostly preclinical data. Activin type II
receptor antagonists are flagged as potentially combining weight loss with muscle and bone
preservation — worth watching as a co-therapy that could change this protocol's premise.

**E3. Henney AE, Wilding JPH, Alam U, Cuthbertson DJ. Obesity pharmacotherapy in older adults:
a narrative review of evidence.**
*International Journal of Obesity.* 2024;49(3):369–380.
[doi:10.1038/s41366-024-01529-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41366-024-01529-z)

Supports the older-adult risk tier. Places emphasis on resistance training for preserving
muscle strength *and* bone mineral density in older adults on anti-obesity medication, and
notes intentional weight loss in this group is effective and safe.

**E4. Nong K, Jeppesen BT, Shi Q, et al. Medications for adults with type 2 diabetes: a living
systematic review and network meta-analysis.**
*BMJ.* 2025;390:e083039.
[doi:10.1136/bmj-2024-083039](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-083039)

493,168 participants across 869 trials. Two points bear on this protocol: tirzepatide produces
the largest weight reduction (MD −8.63 kg, moderate certainty), and tirzepatide and GLP-1RAs
**probably increase severe gastrointestinal events** (tirzepatide OR 4.21, 95% CI 1.87–9.49).
The latter supports the protocol's GI-limiting flag and its phasing around dose escalation.
Note that in this analysis it is thiazolidinediones, not incretins, that increase major
osteoporotic fractures.

## F. Population and care-gap context

**F1. Gallup. In U.S., GLP-1 Usage Reaches New High.** 2026.
[Poll](https://news.gallup.com/poll/712157/glp-usage-reaches-new-high.aspx)

11% of US adults currently using a GLP-1 for weight loss, up from 3% in 2024; 15% ever used.

**F2. PwC. GLP-1 consumer trends in food, apparel and wellness.** 2026.
[Report](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/consumer-markets/library/glp-1-consumer-trends.html)

Source of the behavioural gap: 41% of current users have never done strength training, 21%
increased it, 17% decreased it; 36% increased walking/running/cycling/swimming; 15% use
wearables to manage health on the medication.

**F3. Endocrine Society. Exercise decreases among people taking GLP-1 medication.** ENDO 2026.
[Press release](https://www.endocrine.org/news-and-advocacy/news-room/2026/maharjan-press-release-endo-2026)

Conference presentation — grade accordingly. Reports declining exercise among GLP-1 users, and
associates lower protein intake, older age, and female sex with greater muscle loss on
semaglutide. The origin of the protocol's high-risk phenotype.

## G. Trials in progress — watch these

**G1. NCT07554417** — Combatting Muscle Loss in Obese Adult Patients on GLP-1 Medications
Through Dietary Counseling and Exercise. 12-week exercise and individualized nutrition
program; outcomes include muscle and bone loss, strength, fitness, and function.
[Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07554417)

The closest published trial to this protocol's design. Results should trigger a protocol
revision.

**G2. NCT07702461** — Determination of Resistance Training Status for Patients on
Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists. Ohio State University, observational, n=300,
primary completion October 2026.
[Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07702461)

**G3. NCT06885736 — LEAN-PREP.** *LEAN Mass Preservation With Resistance Exercise and Protein
During Semaglutide and Tirzepatide Therapy.* Dasman Diabetes Institute with the University of
Glasgow. Randomised, four arms — control / protein / resistance exercise / both — n=232,
6-month follow-up, recruiting since August 2025, **primary completion August 2029.**
[Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06885736)

**This is the definitive trial for this protocol's premise, and it is already running.**
Its primary outcome is MRI quadriceps cross-sectional area; its secondary outcomes include
DXA body composition, **grip strength by Jamar dynamometer, chair-rise performance, and
4-metre gait speed** — substantially the battery in §4 of the protocol, arrived at
independently. Eligibility is BMI ≥27, age ≥18, excluding anyone already doing resistance
training or >1 h/week vigorous aerobic activity.

Two implications. First, the protocol's design is externally validated: a research institute
and a Russell Group university converged on the same intervention and nearly the same
measures. Second, it dates the window — **rigorous randomised evidence lands in 2029**, and
until then this remains a practice built on mechanism, consensus cut-points, and smaller
studies. Revise this protocol when LEAN-PREP reports.

## Evidence grading used in this protocol

| Grade | Meaning | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| **Established** | Consensus guideline or meta-analysis | EWGSOP2 cut-points (A1); strength preserved in deficit (C1) |
| **Supported** | Multiple primary studies, consistent direction | Resistance training preserves lean mass in restriction (C2, C4); protein targets (D2) |
| **Emerging** | Limited or conflicting primary data | Optimal resistance volume in restriction (C2 vs C3); GLP-1 bone effects (E1, E2) |
| **Contextual** | Survey, poll, or conference abstract | Population and behaviour figures (F1–F3) |

Nothing in this protocol rests on a single contextual source. Where a threshold derives from
emerging evidence, the protocol errs toward monitoring more closely rather than intervening
more aggressively.
