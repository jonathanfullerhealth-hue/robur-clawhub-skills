# Five Theses — Where This Actually Goes

Prepared for Jonathan Fuller (BScKin, MScPT) — September 2026

The companion documents in this repo are careful. This one is not. Same evidence, bolder
reading. Treat it as a set of bets, not a set of findings.

---

## Thesis 1: The post-GLP-1 body is a clinical category, and it does not have a name yet

Twelve million American adults are on a GLP-1. Thirty to forty percent of what they lose is
fat-free mass. Forty-one percent of them have never lifted anything in their lives.

Run that forward five years. You get millions of people in their forties and fifties who are
thin, and weak, and quietly osteopenic — who look healthy in a mirror and have the
musculoskeletal reserve of someone twenty years older. They will present as a wave, and
the system will have no idea what to call them.

**Name it.** "Post-GLP-1 deconditioning." "Pharmacologic sarcopenia." Whatever you want —
the label matters less than being the person who put it there. Whoever names a clinical
category writes its assessment protocol, its programming standard, and its referral
pathway. That person becomes the citation, and everyone who arrives later cites them.

This is available right now because the wave is two years ahead of the infrastructure and
because naming things requires a credential you happen to hold. It will not be available in
2029.

The tell that this is real: bariatric surgery solved this problem decades ago with
standardized multidisciplinary follow-up. GLP-1s produce comparable weight loss with none of
that scaffolding — only one in five patients gets so much as a dietitian referral. The
playbook exists. Nobody has ported it.

## Thesis 2: Stop selling to people. Sell to payers.

This is the biggest single reframe available to you, and everything else in this repo is
small next to it.

A consumer pays $27 a month and quits in March. A payer signs a contract.

Payers have three properties consumers do not: budgets that are already allocated, mandates
that force them to spend, and renewal cycles that do not depend on anyone's motivation
surviving a bad week. Fire departments under NFPA 1580 are a payer. Workers' compensation
boards funding return-to-work conditioning are a payer. Hospitals bleeding nurses to
shift-work burnout are a payer. Insurers pricing healthspan are a payer. Longevity clinics
buying a protocol library are a payer.

Same knowledge. Same programming. Fifty times the contract value, and it renews on a
purchase order instead of on willpower.

The reason almost nobody does this is that selling to institutions is slow, unglamorous, and
requires credentials. You have the credentials. The slowness is the moat — it is exactly
why the AI-generalist crowd cannot follow you there.

**The pivot in one line: you are not in the coaching business, you are in the
physical-risk-reduction business, and risk is something institutions pay to reduce.**

## Thesis 3: Own data nobody else has, because arguments are now free

AI has made every argument free. Anyone can generate a persuasive case for any training
philosophy in ten seconds, which means persuasion is worth nothing and evidence is worth
everything.

You are sitting on something genuinely rare: longitudinal check-in data on shift-working
endurance athletes, collected through a deterministic triage engine, by a clinician. That
population is understudied, hard to recruit, and central to two of the payer markets above.

**Run the cohort study. Publish it.** Not a blog post — a real one, in a real journal, even
a small one. The moment there is a paper with your name on it about training shift workers,
you stop competing with content creators. You become the thing they cite.

Your `client-checkin-analyzer` was already built rules-first, which means your data is
structured and consistent — the hard part of any prospective cohort, already done by
accident. Almost nobody in the coaching world can say that.

Proprietary evidence is the only moat AI cannot erode, because AI can reason about data it
does not have but it cannot collect it.

## Thesis 4: The intersection nobody is standing at is women, perimenopause, and GLP-1s

Perimenopause costs up to ten percent of bone mineral density and roughly 0.6% of muscle
per year afterward. GLP-1s take another thirty to forty percent of lost weight from lean
mass. Women in their late forties are doing both simultaneously, in enormous numbers, and
being congratulated for it.

That is a double hit to bone and muscle at exactly the life stage where fracture risk starts
compounding, and I could not find anyone treating it as a single problem. The menopause
people are not looking at the drugs. The GLP-1 people are not looking at the life stage.

This is the highest-urgency gap in this document and the one with the shortest window,
because it is obvious in retrospect and someone will see it.

Do not front it alone. Co-lead with a woman clinician and you have a defensible, urgent,
genuinely important thing that no one else is building.

## Thesis 5: As AI eats knowledge work, the premium moves to bodies

Here is the frame that ties the rest together.

Every year, the supply of competent knowledge work goes up and its price goes down. Analysis,
writing, planning, synthesis — all of it is being commoditized in real time. Meanwhile the
supply of people who can assess a body, put hands on it, take responsibility for it, and sign
their licence to that judgment is fixed. It cannot be scaled by a model release.

Scarcity is moving from the knowledge to the embodiment, and from information to
accountability.

Which means: **you are not building an AI business. You are building a body business that
runs on AI.** Your skills are not the product. They are the leverage that lets one licensed
clinician serve fifty people instead of twelve.

That inversion changes what you do next. It says stop trying to compete in the AI category,
where a free group has 425,000 members and the price anchor is seven dollars. It says push
your differentiation toward the licence, the hands, the assessment, the signature — the
things that get more valuable every time a model ships, not less.

The AI-for-coaches group is a fine business. The clinician who used AI to become the standard
of care for a category that did not exist five years ago is a different kind of outcome.

## The bet I would actually make

Thesis 1 and Thesis 2, together, immediately.

Write the post-GLP-1 conditioning protocol. Put your name and letters on it. Sell it to the
clinics prescribing the drugs, not to the people taking them. That is one product, one
credential, one wave, and one buyer with money and a problem they cannot solve internally.

Then run Thesis 3 underneath it — because the clinics you sell to generate the data that
makes the paper that makes you the standard.

Everything else in this repo is a good business. That is a category.
