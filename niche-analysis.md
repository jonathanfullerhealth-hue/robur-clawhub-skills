# Niche Analysis — Is There a Better Group to Run?

Prepared for Jonathan Fuller (BScKin, MScPT) — September 2026

Companion to `community-strategy.md`. Answers two questions: what is already on Skool,
and is there a better niche outside your current domain.

**A caveat that governs every number below.** Public Skool data comes from the trending
and top-1000 lists. That is survivorship bias at full strength — the median *trending*
group looks nothing like the median group. Read these as "what winning looks like," never
as "what to expect."

---

## What is already on Skool

**The market is mostly paid and mostly cheap.** 77.7% of trending communities charge.
Median price is **$27/month**. Median trending community is **205 members**.
([Joe Apfelbaum's top-1000 analysis](https://joeapfelbaum.substack.com/p/skool-communities-analysis-top-1000))

**Price distribution across active paid groups:**

| Band | Share |
|---|---|
| $9–19 | 41% |
| $29–49 | 36% |
| $79–199 | 17% |
| $200+ | 6% |

Median MRR across trending paid Skools is roughly **$2,800–$4,200**, with the top decile
above $25K.
([Communipass](https://communipass.com/blog/skool-revenue-benchmarks-2026/))

**The AI category is a bloodbath at the top.** AI Automation (A–Z) has ~164,744 members at
**$7/month**. AI Automation Society is the largest free group on the platform at roughly
**425,000 members**. AI & Automation is only 9.4% of communities, but the giants have set
the price anchor at nearly zero.
([RevenueGeeks](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/best-communities))

**Do not compete there.** A generalist AI group is a fight against a free group with a
population the size of a mid-size city.

## The finding that actually matters

Price is set by **category**, not by effort.

| Category | Median price | % charging |
|---|---|---|
| Money | $29 | 85.7% |
| Hobbies | $14 | — |
| Developer / affiliate / real estate / crypto | **$97–150** | — |

The high-priced niches share one property: *the knowledge directly translates into the
member making money.* Hobbies price at $14 because getting better at a hobby does not pay
for the membership.
([Codingoblin, 2,600+ groups](https://codingoblin.com/blog/most-profitable-skool-community-niches))

Here is the arbitrage, and it does not require a new domain:

> **Fitness and health content is a $14–29 product. Business operations content is a
> $97–150 product. The same knowledge, sold to the same person in a different role,
> prices at 4–5×.**

"Help you train for your ultra" sells to an athlete for $27. "Run your practice on AI"
sells to that same person, as a clinic owner, for $97+. You do not need a niche you don't
know. You need to stop selling the Hobbies version of what you already know.

## If you still want a cold niche: the entry fee

A community business is a credibility business. In a domain you don't know you have no
stories, no proof, no network, and no answer to the only question that matters — *have you
actually done this?* Low engagement drives 32% of all community churn, and engagement comes
from the leader having real answers.

**The test: can you produce five real case studies in sixty days?** Five free
implementations for real operators, documented, with numbers. If yes, the niche is
enterable. If no, you are an AI generalist competing with a 425,000-member free group.

Ranked honestly against that test:

### Veterinary practices — best pain, worst timing

Over 60% of vets report high exhaustion. Medical records eat **30–40% of the workday**.
**91%** name documentation as where AI helps most, and **98%** are interested in AI for
admin tasks.
([CoVet 2026 survey](https://co.vet/post/covet-2026-veterinary-ai-survey/),
[Digitail](https://digitail.com/blog/how-ai-reduces-veterinary-burnout/))

That is close to the best pain data I found anywhere. **But funded software companies are
already eating it** — CoVet, Digitail, and Shepherd all ship vet-specific AI documentation.
You would be selling education against products that already work. Weaker than the numbers
suggest.

### Cleaning and lawn care businesses — the real gap

HVAC sits at **81.5% AI adoption** and plumbing at 65%, but **cleaning and lawn care lag on
every metric** — lowest AI adoption and lowest pricing confidence at 48%. Meanwhile only
25% of residential contractors use AI meaningfully and **54% plan to invest within 1–3
years**. The average plumbing business does ~$1.28M in revenue and 60%+ of HVAC shops clear
$500K, so there is real money in the sector.
([Jobber 2026 Home Service Trends](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/),
[ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/ai-for-home-service))

This is the largest genuinely underserved gap in anything I looked at: money present,
adoption lowest, intent to buy high, and nobody teaching them. **The cost is that it is
culturally the furthest thing from you.** No shared language, no network, no credibility —
you would be paying the full sixty-day entry fee, and you would not enjoy it.

### Real estate, crypto, affiliate — skip

Highest median prices ($97–150) and the most saturated categories on the platform. You have
no edge and no interest. This is where people go to lose a year.

## Recommendation

**No, there is not a better cold niche. There is a better position in an adjacent one.**

Run the group for **owners of small health and movement practices** — physio, chiro, RMT,
kinesiology, and coaching businesses. Specifically:

- It is a **Money-category product** ($97+ pricing) that you can sell with **Health-category
  credibility you already hold**. That is the arbitrage, captured without a new domain.
- The pain is documented and severe — PTs spend **2–6 hours a day** on documentation — and
  these buyers are already spending on AI.
- Your MScPT makes you a peer, not a vendor. In a cold niche that advantage is exactly zero.
- Your existing skills already serve it. `client-checkin-analyzer`, `content-pipeline-
  generator`, and `coaching-ai-assessment` are generic to any practitioner with clients.
- You can produce the five case studies immediately, because one of them is your own
  business.

Price it at **$97**, not $47. The $47 recommendation in `community-strategy.md` was set for
an audience of solo endurance coaches. Practice owners are a different wallet, and the
category data says the ceiling is 3× higher.

**Keep the endurance coach group as the free tier.** It fills itself from your existing
brand and content, it costs you almost nothing, and the practice owners are already inside
it — coaches who also run clinics are the natural upgrade path.

---

## If you want to test the cold-niche thesis cheaply

Do not commit. Run one experiment: pick **cleaning or lawn care**, find five operators,
offer a free implementation, and see whether you can stand the conversations. Sixty days,
no platform spend, no brand built. If you enjoy it and get results, the gap is real and you
will know. If you don't, you have lost two months instead of a year — and the practice
owner group will have been compounding the whole time.
