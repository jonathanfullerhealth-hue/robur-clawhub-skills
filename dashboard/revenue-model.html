The customer count is the strategy

Our strategy documents recommend a $47/month community for solo coaches. That was right for a content business. We then built clinical infrastructure, and it points at a different buyer with a bigger problem.

The membership route is a media business. It needs an audience, a content machine, and a permanent top of funnel. People run it well and it is a real business — it is not the business the assets we have describe.

02

Who has the money and the problem

The buyer is a medical weight-loss clinic or med spa that prescribes GLP-1s. Patients pay them $300–600 a month. After drug cost, supplies and NP/PA oversight, the clinic keeps roughly $244 per patient per month. Forty active patients is $14,000–24,000 in monthly recurring revenue.

Up to 65% of patients without type 2 diabetes stop GLP-1 therapy within a year. Two-thirds of the lost weight comes back within a year of stopping.
Compounded, that is 8.4% monthly churn and an average patient lifetime of twelve months — about $2,900 in gross margin.

A 40-patient clinic loses 26 patients a year, about 2.2 every month. By year end that is $76,000 of annualised gross margin gone, to be replaced with paid acquisition.

That is the problem we solve. Not muscle loss in the abstract — churn. And the clinic owner is exactly the customer the founding filter describes: capital, a P&L, and a problem whose solution makes them money.

03

What we sell

A conditioning and monitoring programme the clinic runs under its own name, that keeps patients on programme longer and gives the prescriber something defensible to point at. All of it already exists.

The clinic is not buying information. It is buying a retention mechanism with a clinical justification attached — and a report that proves the programme works, which it then uses to sell the programme.

04

Pricing

Take the per-patient model. It is the only one whose incentives point the same way as the clinic's. $25 per active patient per month, $500/month minimum — 10% of their margin on each patient. Which sets the bar the programme has to clear, and the bar is a duration, not a headcount.

The fee pays for itself the moment we extend average time on programme by 11.4%. About six weeks.

Without us the clinic keeps $244/month for twelve months: $2,913. With us they keep $219/month, so they need 13.3 months to be level. Everything past six extra weeks is theirs.

If the programme moves annual discontinuation from 65% to 55% — the number the pilot has to establish — average lifetime goes to 15.5 months and the clinic nets about $490 more gross margin per patient against $388 of fees. A 1.3× return on the fee, plus a quarterly outcomes report they can market with.

That is not a spectacular multiple and should not be sold as one. It is a defensible one, and it survives a clinic owner with a spreadsheet, which the alternative framings do not. Price on their margin, not our effort — and be honest that the whole case rests on a retention number nobody has measured yet.

Why we win against what exists

The space is not empty. It is occupied by three shapes of competitor, none of which is the shape we are.

Two differentiators are technical rather than positioning.

We refuse to triage on bioimpedance

Every scanner-led programme in this market triages on BIA lean mass, which over-reads fat-free mass loss in dehydrated patients — and this population is chronically dehydrated. Our skill bars it explicitly and triages on grip strength and function instead. That is defensible in front of a physician in a way an InBody trend line is not.

The triage is deterministic and tested

Thirty-three tests over the escalation rules, and a status that reports incomplete rather than quietly clearing a patient nobody measured. That is the difference between a decision aid a medical director signs off on and one they do not.

06

The first 90 days

The bottleneck is not product. It is that we have zero patients, zero outcome data and no reference customer. Nothing else matters until that changes.

Days 1–30

Build the intake, get one clinic

Close the write path from the client app into the check-ins table; the pipeline is otherwise finished and idle. Then land a single pilot. Free for 90 days in exchange for the data and a named reference. One clinic, not five.

Days 31–60

Run the protocol on real patients

Every check-in through the triage engine, every escalation logged. This is the first time any of it touches a real person, and it will surface things the sample cohort cannot.

Days 61–90

Produce the report and convert

The quarterly report is the sales asset. Retention against the clinic's own prior cohort is the number that sells the second clinic, and the second sells the tenth.

Ten clinics at $500–1,000/month is $60,000–120,000 ARR and a repeatable pitch. That is the point at which this is a business rather than a bet.

Where the community comes back

Clinics that buy the programme need somebody to deliver it. That is the second side of the market, and it is what the community strategy is actually for: certify coaches into the protocol at $997, place them into clinics that have licensed it. The group stops being the revenue engine and becomes the supply of practitioners — far easier to sell to a coach than another membership, because it ends in paid work.

07

What kills this

The window closes in 2029

LEAN-PREP (NCT06885736) reports in August 2029. When it does, this stops being a proprietary protocol and becomes a guideline anyone can implement. Three years to build the customer base, the outcome data and the brand. After that we compete on execution, not on knowing something.

Clinical liability is a different business to selling courses

Decision support sold into medical practices carries obligations a community does not. The scope boundary in the protocol is the mitigation and must never soften: we programme exercise, the prescriber owns medication, the prescriber or dietitian owns nutrition. Get this reviewed before the first paid clinic, not after.

No proof yet

Every figure in section 4 is a projection built on the clinic's published economics, not on our results. The pilot exists to replace the projection with evidence. If the protocol does not improve retention the business does not work — and we should find that out in 90 days on one clinic rather than in two years on forty.
